## 3. Blocking finding B1 — wrapper moves the external effect past the reviewed final timing gate

**Locations:** `A/wiggles-submission-v1.mjs:271-298,302-309`; `A/reused/bounded-rebroadcast-v1.mjs:83,171-180,220-225`; evidence validator at `A/wiggles-submission-v1.mjs:203-223`.

### Cause and reachable send scenario

The reviewed scheduler prepares and persists a send request, checks `clock.now() - origin < latest`, and immediately invokes `prepared.dispatch` (`reused/bounded-rebroadcast-v1.mjs:222`). Its existing test explicitly requires that final clock read to be adjacent to external dispatch (`reused/bounded-rebroadcast-v1.test.mjs:45`); the direct-fetch preparation test at line 119 similarly checks that dispatch begins with the effect.

The new composition passes `boundedTransport` as that dispatch function. It has no `prepare`/`dispatch` split. Its body then:

1. Awaits durable episode inspection and another verified wire read (lines 273-274), including real file/directory fsyncs in `sqlite-decision-authority-v1.mjs:421-471`.
2. Checks the wrapper-wide limit and wall deadline (lines 275-277).
3. Writes and fsyncs a new call reservation, then fsyncs its directory and reads it back (lines 281-283; persistence helper lines 46-61).
4. Rechecks only the **wrapper overall** full-timeout-fit condition (line 285), creates timeout machinery, and invokes the actual injected transport in a later promise microtask (lines 286-294).

There is no post-wrapper check of the scheduler's acknowledgment-relative exclusive latest-start boundary. The request summary carries relative window metadata, but this function neither retains the acknowledgment origin nor enforces that window.

A normal slow filesystem operation suffices; no hostile administrator or transport is needed. Suppose ordinal 2 passes the scheduler's final gate at acknowledgment-relative 2999 ms. The added authority read or journal fsync returns at 3000 ms. The default 190000-ms overall wrapper budget still admits the full 2000-ms send timeout, so the external callback begins at the excluded boundary. The same defect applies to ordinal 3 at 6000 ms. Slow I/O can be rejected once it returns; no ability to interrupt a blocked kernel fsync is necessary to enforce this invariant. Therefore the closeout's cooperative-I/O caveat does not excuse this send.

The wire remains identical and the send count remains bounded, but **the approved timing contract is violated**. A later timeout, cancellation, or unresolved result cannot undo a callback already begun outside the permitted window.

### Related deadline manifestations of the same misplaced gate

- The resolver checks its own 180000-ms full-timeout-fit budget before invoking transport (`reused/bounded-rebroadcast-v1.mjs:174,177,179-180`). Newly inserted wrapper reads/persistence consume time after that check. For example, a 2000-ms read admitted at resolution-relative 178000 ms can reach external transport at 178001 ms after wrapper work. The full reviewed timeout no longer fits, while the distinct 190000-ms wrapper budget may still admit it. The wrapper does not know or recheck the resolver's absolute deadline.
- `clock.unixSeconds() >= c.deadline_unix_seconds` is checked before call-journal persistence (line 277), but not after it. Crossing the runtime wall deadline during that persistence also permits a callback after the configured deadline.

These are consequences of the same integration boundary, not demands to redesign the previously reviewed scheduler.

### Why current evidence and tests do not detect it

The journal records `elapsed_ms` before its own persistence. Validation checks nondecreasing elapsed values, method/timeout identity, send count, request IDs, and fit under the **overall wrapper** timeout. It does not reconstruct acknowledgment-relative dispatch time, resolution-origin fit, or the wall deadline at actual dispatch. The scheduler records approved window constants, not the newly delayed external-start observation. A late-window send can consequently have internally consistent retained records rather than necessarily failing completeness validation.

The positive runtime test has immediate injected transport and clock advances primarily through sleep. The new `deadline` case reduces the overall wrapper limit to 1 ms and refuses before any send. The late-response tests advance time or settle after dispatch. None injects elapsed time into the new authority-read/call-journal interval after the reviewed final gate. The unchanged scheduler adjacency test exercises the scheduler directly and cannot certify adjacency through this new wrapper.

### Essential regression gaps

Under separate repair authorization, use the actual trusted runtime composition to inject delay into the added wire/authority read and call-journal persistence, without changing the reviewed scheduler tests:

- For both rebroadcast ordinals, cross the exclusive window boundary after the scheduler gate and require **zero external callback for the disallowed send**, conservative consumed authority, and no later catch-up or replacement signing.
- Cross the resolver's remaining-full-timeout-fit boundary for status, block-height, and exact-transaction calls; require no newly disallowed external call and conservative terminal handling.
- Cross the configured runtime wall deadline during journal persistence; require no callback afterward.
- Require evidence validation not to certify an out-of-policy dispatch, and distinguish durable possible-dispatch reservations from callbacks refused before effect.

The final applicable timing gates must govern the actual external effect after the newly introduced work, not merely entry into an asynchronous wrapper. No repair or reproduction was executed in this review.

## 4. Other reviewed boundaries

### Attempts, concurrency, revocation, and crash/restart

`recordSubmissionPossibleV1` is a transactional stage check/update, not an idempotent grant of another schedule (`A/sqlite-decision-authority-v1.mjs:989-1009`). The wrapper proceeds only after that transition succeeds; a losing invocation returns unresolved. A preexisting `SUBMISSION_POSSIBLE` or `FINALIZED` row enters verification only (`A/wiggles-submission-v1.mjs:246-258`). Partial files cannot recreate a pre-submission row or replenish unused sends.

The whole schedule is deliberately consumed before its first possible effect. Dense exclusive-create journal reservations precede external callbacks, and the in-memory cumulative send cap is three. Lost initial acknowledgment cancels rebroadcast while allowing exact-signature resolution; it does not return signing authority. Wrapper callback refusals can leave scheduler attempt retention without matching journal entries, which complete validation rejects rather than certifying as actual dispatch.

The new recovery tests contain genuine SIGKILL checkpoints before transport and after injected effect/before acknowledgment, and separate-process submission election (`A/wiggles-submission-recovery-v1.test.mjs:23-109`). They verify a consumed `SUBMISSION_POSSIBLE` stage, identical retained signature/wire, and no renewed transport. Ordinary finalization reopen tests are not mislabeled as four new whole-runtime finalization crash probes.

The narrow revocation repair at `A/sqlite-decision-authority-v1.mjs:907-912` preserves an already submission-resolving predecessor instead of applying the signed-intent transition twice. The transaction still verifies the wire and applies authenticated revocation; the earlier admitted-state branch still performs the signed transition. First-submission election checks revocation, subsequent send callbacks inspect it, and reads may continue for escaped bytes. Valid terminal/source evidence may close a revoked leg without blessing an incomplete transmission journal. B1 remains the identified final dispatch-gate defect.
