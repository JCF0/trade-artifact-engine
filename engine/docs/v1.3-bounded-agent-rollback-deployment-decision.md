# Bounded-agent rollback authority: deployment decision note

## Status and scope

**Proposed design only. No deployment choice has been approved. The tested offline checkpoint remains live-blocked.**

This note records the completed bounded assessment, not a new review or implementation. The external authority described below does not exist in the inspected implementation. This note does not change implementation, authorize credentials or infrastructure, or refresh the existing source manifest. It is not covered by that manifest.

Passing the completed four-case finalization crash matrix closes only that offline coverage gap. Valid-database rollback detection remains unimplemented. Production signing integration and the other live-readiness requirements remain blocked. Synthetic evidence does not establish live onchain occurrence; agent provenance never supplies economic authority.

## 1. Recommendation and exact trust assumptions

Recommend one independently administered, durable, append-only generation/head authority outside the Wiggles host's snapshot and restore domain. SQLite remains the local authority store. An authority-bearing transition requires local/remote agreement and remote compare-and-set advancement before local commit and capability release.

The smallest proposed recovery policy is conservative: **block on divergent state rather than automatically rewind, reconstruct, or replay execution.** This trades availability and operator effort for a smaller safety-critical recovery implementation.

### Current protection versus missing freshness

The local implementation rejects missing, blank, corrupt, valid-empty, malformed-schema, and internally inconsistent databases; invalid authority bindings; inconsistent state/ordinal/revocation records; and required signed bytes that are absent or altered. It checks relevant file ownership, permissions, type, bounds, and observed identity. Ordinary process restart does not recreate consumed authority.

It cannot distinguish an internally valid older database, with matching retained files, from current state. Such restoration can erase a reservation, revocation, or execution checkpoint. Explicit administrative reprovisioning after deletion is not independently prevented from reusing the same authorization. A checksum, marker restored with the database, or filesystem permissions alone does not solve this problem.

### Principals and trust

- **Hermes:** authenticates bounded decisions only. It has no database access, remote-head mutation credential, provisioning authority, or signing capability.
- **Wiggles runtime:** trusted reviewed code enforcing typed transitions and capability ordering. A dedicated identity can advance an already-enrolled namespace, but cannot create, delete, reset, or rebind it. A compromised executor is outside this proposed rollback-only guarantee.
- **Provisioning administrator:** enrolls the immutable authorization/episode binding and provisions the matching initial database. Local restoration does not grant permission to reset or replace the remote authority.
- **Remote authority administrator:** independently controls the service, storage, trust configuration, and recovery. The local restore operator must not control its reset or backup-restoration capability.

The remote namespace binds the mandate, authorization, episode, and executor release. Permanent enrollment/tombstone records prevent reuse of the same authorization under a newly created namespace. Local configuration restoration must not silently select another service or trust root.

The guarantee assumes that the remote head is linearizable, durably acknowledged, and not silently rolled back. Protection against an administrator controlling both domains, malicious executor code, or an all-powerful host/cloud administrator is not claimed. Stronger protection would require a separately approved threat model and mechanism.

### Previously inspected deployment evidence

The completed targeted inspection did not establish a suitable existing mechanism on the inspected repository host. It found no relevant system service units, no TPM devices/device nodes, and no named Wiggles/Hermes/Artifact local runtime accounts among the checked account names. An `ops` account exists. An empty TPM class directory is not a usable TPM.

This is not proof that no remote or workstation service exists. Those environments, administrative controls, and recovery guarantees remain **unresolved**. No product has been qualified.

## 2. Remote record and complete commit ordering

The proposed remote record contains:

- Immutable namespace and mandate/authorization/episode/release bindings.
- Monotonically increasing generation and previous-head digest.
- Unique transition identifier and closed transition kind.
- Canonical successor-authority digest covering authority-bearing episode, ordinal, challenge, and revocation records, including retained signed-wire identities.

Hash canonical logical authority, not physical SQLite/WAL bytes. Retain append-only history and enrollment/tombstones. The service offers authenticated, non-cached current-head reads and atomic CAS append; it acknowledges an append only after durable persistence. These are **requirements, not implemented mechanisms**.

Apply the following protocol to every authority-bearing mutation, including reservation, preparation, challenge registration, revocation, signed-state updates, submission markers, and finalization:

1. Serialize local authority access and begin a local transaction. Do not interleave other operations on its database handle while awaiting the bounded remote operation.
2. Reconstruct and validate local authority. Read the current remote head through the authenticated freshness path. Require exact namespace, generation, and canonical authority-digest agreement. Missing records, conflicts, stale responses, or unavailable authority block execution.
3. Validate the existing typed transition against its predecessor. Compute the exact successor, its canonical digest, and a unique bound transition identifier. For signed-intent persistence, first verify and durably retain the exact signed wire and its directory entry; an orphan file grants no authority.
4. Atomically CAS-append the successor to the remote head using the exact expected predecessor. The service must reject a conflicting predecessor, namespace, binding, or reuse of the transition identifier. Its history must not permit resetting the namespace.
5. Require an unambiguous durable acknowledgment bound to that exact transition and successor. A timeout or lost acknowledgment is not evidence of absence and grants no capability. Do not automatically retry execution. An authenticated lookup may establish what was recorded, but cannot independently authorize replay.
6. Commit the exact successor and new generation to SQLite in the local transaction. If local commit fails or its outcome is uncertain, block; never rewind the remote head to match local storage.
7. Only after both commits are confirmed may the current operation return success or release its associated capability. An exact duplicate request can identify the retained result but cannot confer a fresh right to sign or send.

### Capability-specific ordering

- Reservation completes both commits before preparation proceeds.
- `PREPARED` commits the exact prepared commitment before the key-load transition.
- `KEY_LOAD_STARTED_AMBIGUOUS` completes both commits before signer invocation. Restart at this checkpoint must never infer that signing did not happen.
- Returned signed bytes are verified and made durable before advancing `SIGNED_INTENT_DURABLE`. Retention during an outage is conservative evidence preservation, not permission to release or submit bytes.
- `SUBMISSION_POSSIBLE` completes both commits before first submission. Its persistence means bytes may have escaped even if the sending process later reports nothing.
- Revocation uses the same head and serialization. If revocation wins before key-load or first-submission advancement, that advancement is blocked. If key-load or submission advancement wins first, retain the existing ambiguity/resolution semantics; revocation cannot retract work already allowed to escape.
- Finalization advances the remote head and commits its coupled ordinal/episode successor locally. It never generates replacement execution authority.

Bounded RPC deadlines are required. Their exact values and client lookup/retry policy remain **unresolved**. Integration with any future live identical-byte scheduler is also **unresolved**; no scheduler modification or activation is approved here.

## 3. Crash windows and fail-closed outcomes

1. **Before validation or before either update:** no new authority is granted. A later request must independently revalidate current local and remote state.
2. **Local transaction started, remote CAS not issued:** uncommitted local work rolls back. No associated preparation, signing, or submission capability is released.
3. **Remote CAS issued, outcome unknown:** it may have committed. Timeout, disconnect, lost acknowledgment, or client crash causes a stop. No local success claim or capability release; no assumption that retrying execution is safe.
4. **Remote CAS definitely rejected:** roll back local work and stop. A conflicting current head is not permission to rebase the action automatically.
5. **Remote append committed, acknowledgment lost:** the remote head may lead SQLite. Reopening detects disagreement and blocks. Exact operation lookup can classify the record, but does not automatically repair local state or replay the capability.
6. **Remote acknowledgment received, local commit not started or interrupted:** remote authority leads local state, or local commit outcome is uncertain. On reopen, compare validated state with the remote head. Any mismatch blocks. Do not decrement the remote generation.
7. **Local commit completed, response lost to the caller:** the transition remains consumed. An exact replay may report the retained result; it must not trigger another capability invocation.
8. **Both commits completed, process died before capability invocation:** preserve the consumed checkpoint. At key-load or submission checkpoints this remains ambiguous despite the possibility that no external action occurred. Do not automatically sign or send again.
9. **Signer invocation started, no signed result durably retained:** key-load ambiguity remains. No replacement signing. Missing wire cannot be reconstructed from a head digest.
10. **Signed wire retained, signed-intent head/local update incomplete:** retain the orphan or partially recorded evidence. Release and first submission remain blocked. Do not manufacture a replacement intent or signature.
11. **Submission checkpoint committed, send or acknowledgment interrupted:** bytes may have escaped. No fresh transaction, rebuild, requote, blockhash refresh, or replacement signing. Only separately authorized exact-byte/read-only reconciliation may resolve the ambiguity; this note does not authorize it.
12. **Finalization remote append or local coupled commit interrupted:** apply the same mismatch/uncertain-commit stop rules. Do not replay acquisition or disposal. If both stores agree after normal reopening, retain the finalized state rather than recreating authority.
13. **Revocation update interrupted or acknowledgment lost:** do not falsely acknowledge completed revocation. Its remote record may already prevent later advancement; uncertainty or disagreement stops subsequent execution. If key-load/submission already won, escaped work retains its conservative resolution requirement.
14. **Remote service becomes unavailable:** no new authoritative readiness, reservation, key load, first submission, or automatic rebroadcast from cached freshness. Preserve any returned signed bytes without releasing them. Already in-flight work cannot be recalled.
15. **Local backup restored or an older clone started:** remote/local head mismatch blocks. The stale instance cannot win CAS against the current head. Local deletion and reprovisioning must not bypass permanent remote enrollment.

The minimal design intentionally does not promise automatic progress after divergence. A separately verified matching backup may be restored; otherwise the episode remains blocked. A detailed operator recovery and evidence-retention procedure is required before deployment and remains **unimplemented**.

## 4. Freshness of the remote authority itself

An append-only API and valid checksums do not prove that the service's own storage is current. This distinction is essential.

### Ordinary process restart

Required mechanism: the persistence backend acknowledges only durable commits and recovers all acknowledged head advances after process crash. The service must validate its recovered history and head before serving reads or CAS requests. If the backend cannot establish that it recovered acknowledged state, it must not claim readiness.

Durable transactions and log validation address ordinary crash recovery and corruption. They do **not** by themselves detect replacement with an older internally valid backend snapshot. The backend, storage stack, acknowledgment contract, and restart readiness checks have not been selected or implemented.

### Backup restoration, storage loss, or remote host rollback

Required administrator policy: an older backup must never be promoted under the existing trusted service identity as though it were the current authority. Restoration of the authority is separate from local Wiggles restoration, and runtime/local restore credentials cannot request it.

Before serving an existing namespace after remote restoration, current head position must be established against evidence outside that restored snapshot's rollback domain. Possible backend guarantees or independent recovery evidence have **not been established** by the completed assessment. A marker or checksum restored with the service cannot supply this evidence.

If current position cannot be established, the service must remain unavailable and affected episodes remain blocked. Creating a fresh authority namespace or reenrolling an existing authorization is not a recovery shortcut.

An operational stop policy is not an implemented rollback detector. Preventing silent remote restore requires enforceable administrative controls and/or a persistence backend with an accepted independent freshness guarantee. The exact enforcement mechanism is **unresolved and a deployment approval prerequisite**. This design must not be described as end-to-end rollback-safe until that prerequisite is resolved.

A privileged administrator able to restore both authority domains, replace their trust configuration, and bypass the stop policy remains outside the stated guarantee. No infinite chain of external anchors or protection against an all-powerful administrator is proposed.

## 5. Minimum capabilities and managed-service alternative

Required capabilities:

- A host/storage domain independent of Wiggles snapshots and local restore credentials.
- Linearizable current-head reads and conditional append/CAS, with durable acknowledgment and no stale replica/cache acceptance.
- Unique immutable enrollment bindings, permanent tombstones, and retained history sufficient to identify exact prior transitions.
- Authenticated, integrity-protected transport and distinct runtime-advance, enrollment, and administrative permissions.
- Runtime identities unable to delete, reset, overwrite history, rebind namespaces, or change trusted service identity.
- Bounded requests, explicit uncertainty handling, fail-closed startup/outage behavior, and monitoring of unavailability and disagreement.
- A documented, enforceable remote restart/backup-restoration contract that does not silently serve an older head.

A managed persistence service could satisfy these requirements without a custom server **if** its documented and verified semantics provide the required reads, conditional writes, atomic history/head updates, privilege restrictions, and independent recovery guarantee. Direct client access would also need sufficiently narrow policies to enforce the namespace and mutation contract; otherwise a small validating service remains necessary.

No managed product has been assessed or approved. Cost, regions, consistency guarantees, restore behavior, IAM expressiveness, and credential delivery remain **unresolved**. A custom service has explicit operating costs: another security boundary, host/storage lifecycle, authenticated transport, monitoring, recovery ownership, and maintenance. Managed persistence substitutes vendor dependency and service charges; it does not automatically remove the freshness or administrator-trust problem.

## 6. Decisions required before implementation

1. Identify the actual Wiggles deployment host and who controls its snapshots, backups, runtime, and provisioning.
2. Name the independent remote-authority owner and establish whether that owner is administratively separate from the local restore operator.
3. Approve the threat boundary: local rollback protection with trusted executor and non-rolled-back remote authority, excluding an administrator controlling both domains.
4. Choose managed persistence or an independently hosted service, subject to evidence for the required consistency, durability, authorization, and recovery capabilities. No product selection is implied here.
5. Specify how remote freshness is established after its own restart and especially after backup restoration. Accept an enforceable no-resume policy when freshness cannot be proved, or supply a stronger mechanism. This unresolved decision blocks a claim of complete deployment protection.
6. Approve runtime versus enrollment permissions, immutable namespace enrollment/tombstone policy, trust-root custody, and credential-delivery arrangements. No credentials are requested by this note.
7. Accept fail-closed outages and potentially indefinite episode blocking after divergent commits; choose the operator recovery owner, evidence retention, and backup restrictions.
8. Set bounded RPC deadlines, lookup/retry policy, availability objectives, and operational budget.
9. Separately authorize an implementation scope and finite offline acceptance plan. Any live signer/scheduler composition remains a later authorization and release gate.

The previously proposed implementation scope would introduce a head contract/client and, only if required by the chosen backend, a validating service; integrate generation/digest ordering into the SQLite authority; add narrowly scoped ingress failure propagation and deterministic crash/rollback tests; and update documentation and the source manifest in that later authorized pass. None of those changes is approved by creation of this note.
