## Exact input, attempt and recovery behavior

Submission takes only ordinal 1 or 2 through the trusted executor interface. It obtains the retained ordinal, challenge and capture from durable authority, reconstructs the prepared semantic/message commitment from the retained capture, and reconciles mandate, authorization-derived episode, phase, admission, ordinal, prepared digest, semantic digest, message, signature, signed-intent digest and wire identity. The blockhash lifetime/context comes from the retained, digest-bound `getLatestBlockhash` response, not Hermes or fresh RPC.

The authority independently rereads and verifies the retained wire before scheduler entry and again before every potential external call. The exact wire is compared to the initial verified copy. No signer is invoked by this layer. No requote, replacement message, refreshed blockhash, changed fee, new signature, new ordinal, cleanup action or alternative transport architecture exists here. The tests remove the disposable key file after signing and still exercise submission/recovery successfully.

`recordSubmissionPossibleV1` is the existing transactional owner election. Only its winner may start one schedule. Its durable `SUBMISSION_POSSIBLE` checkpoint consumes the **whole** schedule before any external effect, deliberately sacrificing unused opportunities rather than trying to recover a fresh monotonic timer after a crash. Competing/repeated/restarted invocations never call transport again. They validate complete retained evidence if available, otherwise return `UNRESOLVED` with no economic promotion and supervised reconciliation required.

The winner exclusively creates a private submission directory, persists its authority binding and signed-intent record, and uses the reviewed evidence writer for exact requests/responses. Before each external callback it exclusively writes and fsyncs a dense `call-NNNN.json` reservation containing request identity/hash, method kind, ordering, elapsed time, timeout and cumulative send count. File and parent-directory durability precede the callback. A reservation proves possible dispatch, not that the remote provider received it: a crash after intent persistence but before callback may consume budget without an external effect.

Every call requires its full reviewed timeout to fit the remaining overall budget. Persistence is followed by another time check. Per-call timers pass an AbortSignal to the injected transport and discard late outcomes; an absolute outer timer also bounds an abort-ignoring injected sleep. After closure, guarded evidence writes/clock operations refuse late continuation. The trusted injected monotonic clock is checked for safe nonnegative nondecreasing values; the runtime deadline is checked before dispatch. These are application/cooperative-I/O guarantees, not an ability to interrupt a blocked kernel fsync or synchronous malicious same-UID code.

Lost acknowledgment remains possibly transmitted. Uncertain call/retention state never becomes “nothing was sent.” A wrapper refusal can leave a scheduler callback-attempt record without a matching external-dispatch journal; the complete transmission validator rejects that mismatch and the returned result remains `UNRESOLVED`. On an ordinary caught failure the owner attempts a closed `unresolved-outcome.json` diagnostic. If storage itself fails or SIGKILL occurs, that diagnostic may be absent: the already committed SQLite consumption plus partial retained files still prevent a new schedule. No reconstruction/reset of missing authoritative state occurs.

New real process-kill coverage waits for a machine-readable checkpoint, observes SIGKILL, and reopens after (a) durable submission election before transport and (b) an injected external effect before acknowledgment. Separate runtime processes additionally compete for one shared schedule and produce only three identical mock-send records. Existing SQLite/control-plane crash and coupled-finalization tests are reused for their unchanged transitions; they are not relabeled as new whole-runtime terminal crash coverage. The new normal/revoked acquisition/disposal source-closure integration uses ordinary reopen, not a claim of four new end-to-end finalization SIGKILL probes.

## Terminal and economic authority

Acknowledgment is provider receipt of a request, never finalized chain occurrence. The reviewed resolver retains exact-signature finalized status, block-height and exact-transaction observations and classifies finalized success, finalized failure, expired absence or ambiguity. This integration additionally requires explicit finalized status, consistent finalized slots/context and canonically identical status/transaction errors before accepting finalized success/failure. A non-finalized error or conflicting non-null errors cannot be promoted.

`EXPIRED_ABSENT` requires the reviewed combination of adequate exact-signature absence and finalized expiry evidence. Blockhash expiry alone, null status alone, timeout, malformed/mismatched transaction bytes, or unavailable exact-transaction evidence is not absence proof. These observations remain conditional on provider completeness/finality and are not proof against a dishonest provider.

Submission/terminal classifications themselves carry `economic_authority: NOT_PROMOTED`. Definitive failure, expired absence and unresolved submission do not manufacture an acquired quantity, authorize disposal, or free an ordinal. There is no automatic replacement execution after any of these outcomes.

Trusted `finalizeRetainedIntentV1` independently validates the retained terminal subtree against the durable intent, then recaptures a **separate** v1.3 source-authoritative context through trusted injected source capabilities. It requires exact signature, slot, block time, wallet and mint correspondence, successful residual-free effects, coherent opening/ending inventory and exact input quantities. It derives the finalized leg evidence itself; Hermes cannot supply quantities or finalized evidence. Economic context and the derived finalized leg are retained separately in `economic-evidence-<digest>.json` before the existing atomic economic transition. Reuse of an existing economic record re-fsyncs its file, compares exact bytes and fsyncs its directory. Repeated closure recaptures/revalidates source identity; an already-finalized row is not a bypass for substituted source evidence.

Acquisition uses `captureAuthoritativeAcquisitionClosureV1` and the existing branded `closeAcquisitionFromFinalizedEvidenceV1` transition. Disposal input remains exclusively the persisted finalized acquisition quantity; the captured disposal opening-basis digest must equal its persisted acquisition evidence digest. The source-derived target debit must consume that exact quantity and end at zero. The existing `recordFinalizedV1` disposal transition atomically preserves acquisition authority and closes disposal. The two-semantic-swap limit and v1.3 economics are unchanged.

Revocation before submission prevents first dispatch while retaining signed bytes. Revocation after possible transmission forbids subsequent sends but does not erase actual occurrence. A validated terminal subtree and separate source context may still close the designated revoked acquisition/disposal state even when withheld rebroadcasts leave transmission provenance incomplete. This does **not** certify that incomplete scheduler journal. Without complete retained terminal evidence, restart stays unresolved; this slice does not start a fresh read schedule or implement an operator evidence-import/reset mechanism. Further supervised exact-signature evidence acquisition requires its separately bounded authorization.

## Evidence validation and provenance limits

`validateRetainedSubmissionEvidenceV1` accepts a trusted expected binding, not authority inferred from the artifact's own digest. It verifies closed root inventory, canonical binding/signed-intent bytes, both existing scheduler/terminal manifests and validators, completion hashes, every dense journal member/hash, strictly increasing request IDs, timeout/method matching, call/send budgets, actual callback-retention cardinality and terminal consistency. Missing, altered, mismatched, extra or inconsistent records cannot be accepted as complete transmission evidence. Correctly rehashing an invalid journal timeout still fails semantic reconstruction.

Incomplete packages deliberately fail complete verification. `unresolved-outcome.json` is a bounded local diagnostic, not independently authenticated terminal evidence. A verifier must retain the durable authority binding and preserve unresolved disposition when that diagnostic or other required files are missing; no digest proves an absent external effect.

Provenance is trusted executor custody, private filesystem/SQLite authority, exact retained transport observations, and the independently admitted source capabilities used for economic reconstruction. **Self-generated hashes are content identities, not provider signatures or independent source authentication.** A verifier supplied an attacker-controlled “expected binding” has not established authority. Provider truth/completeness and executor/host custody remain explicit trust assumptions. Private 0700/0600 directories/files do not resist malicious same-UID code or a trusted administrator restoring an older internally valid snapshot.

Offline source contexts are explicitly synthetic and rebound to disposable transaction signatures; they are not claimed to be live provider attestations or the real final-proof transaction. New transport wiring has no credential input, no real endpoint URL and no implicit fetch/RPC path. Errors do not persist arbitrary thrown exception details. Agent decision/provenance records, transmission custody observations and source-authoritative economic evidence remain separate.
